⚠️ Identified Risks & Recommended Fixes

Clear explanations of the 3 legal exposure points and how to fix them before signing.

1 Critical 1 Moderate 1 Advisory
🔴 Risk 1: Unlimited Financial Liability for Patent/IP Lawsuits
CRITICAL EXPOSURE
Risk Assessment: Under Section 14.3, intellectual property lawsuits are excluded from the 2× liability cap. If someone claims the software infringes a patent, the vendor has to pay unlimited damages with no ceiling. A single patent lawsuit could cost $10M–$20M and bankrupt the company.
How to Fix It: Add a secondary "super-cap" limiting maximum payout to $5,000,000 USD or 5× annual fees.
Original Contract Clause:
"14.3 Exclusions. The limitations of liability set forth in Section 14.1 shall not apply to damages, legal fees, or settlements resulting from Vendor's indemnification obligations under Section 12 (Intellectual Property)."
Recommended Change to Paste:
"...provided however that Vendor's aggregate indemnity liability under Section 12 shall not exceed $5,000,000 USD or 5× annual fees paid."
🟡 Risk 2: Customer Can Cancel Immediately With Zero Notice
MODERATE RISK
Risk Assessment: Section 8.2 allows the customer to cancel the contract instantly if any regulator sends an inquiry or question about the vendor. It gives 0 days to explain or fix it.
How to Fix It: Require 30 days prior written notice and give the vendor a chance to clarify or resolve the inquiry.
Original Contract Clause:
"8.2 Immediate Termination. Customer reserves the unilateral right to immediately terminate this Agreement without penalty upon receipt of any regulatory inquiry or subpoena regarding Vendor."
Recommended Change to Paste:
"...Customer may terminate only after delivering written notice detailing the inquiry and allowing Vendor thirty (30) days to clarify or remediate the finding."
🔵 Risk 3: Unrealistic 48-Hour Incident Notification Window
ADVISORY
Risk Assessment: Section 11.4 forces the vendor to report any suspected security event within 48 hours. Standard law (GDPR) gives 72 hours and only applies to confirmed breaches. 48 hours on mere suspicion causes premature false alarms.
How to Fix It: Change to the industry-standard 72 hours for confirmed incidents involving personal data.
Original Contract Clause:
"11.4 Incident Reporting. Vendor shall notify Customer in writing within forty-eight (48) hours of detecting any suspected or unverified access to customer data."
Recommended Change to Paste:
"...within seventy-two (72) hours of confirming an actual security incident involving unauthorized access to unencrypted personal data."
